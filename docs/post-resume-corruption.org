#+TITLE: Kernel pages zeroed after xhci0 comes back from a TCSS power loss
#+DATE: 2026-10-30

* Symptom
Three panics on kernel #208 (boot entry "206"), each in a different
subsystem, each soon after the laptop came back from s2idle or after USB-C
was switched on:

| dump | panic                                                      | victim                         |
|------+------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------|
| 21   | trap in vmem_xfree_bt during a Firefox core dump           | a vmem boundary tag (NULL)     |
| 22   | "locking against myself" after a fault in uvm_unmap_remove | Firefox's vm_map entry list    |
| 23   | gefs: assertion "off >= start" (blk.c:411, logappend)      | gefs arena free-range AVL node |

Dump 20 (10-20, #186) is unrelated: a context-creation failure under the
GEM buffer leak, no resume involved.

* Evidence: whole 4 KB pages overwritten with zeros
Read with the rebuilt #208 debug kernel (/var/crash/netbsd.gdb.208; the
original was overwritten by the #209 build, rebuilt from the tree minus
kernel 209's lwp.h/kern_synch.c; .rodata is shifted by the version string,
data structures are exact) and =gdb target kvm=.

- Dump 22: Firefox's map has 8036 entries; entry #1202 (0xffffb39859fcf200)
  is all zeros, its predecessor still points to it.  The whole page
  (PA 0x134bcf000, direct map) is zero.  Its vm_page is an ordinary
  allocated pool page (BUSY|FAKE|CLEAN, no owner, not PG_FREE), and the
  neighbouring vm_map_entry pool pages still link it into the pool's page
  list (in-page headers at offset 0).  So the page allocator never handed it
  out twice: something wrote 4096 zero bytes over it.  No other reference to
  the page anywhere in the dump (vm_page pointer, PA, PFN, PTE-shaped value).
- Dump 23: blkalloc took the highest free range of the arena; that node
  read off=0 len=-16384 (0 minus one block), giving block -16384.  The node's
  page (PA 0x3166ae000) is zero except the two fields later tree operations
  wrote (parent pointer, len).
- Dump 21: a NULL boundary tag in vmem, consistent with the same wipe.

* Correlation: xhci0 cold recovery, not the s2idle resume as such
| dump | "xhci0: resume: power was lost (ACPI), cold recovery" | panic   | gap   |
|------+--------------------------------------------------------+---------+-------|
| 21   | 80178 (at runtime, no s2idle)                          | 80214   | 36 s  |
| 22   | 70276 (s2idle resume)                                  | 70435   | 159 s |
| 23   | 24583 (s2idle resume)                                  | 24630   | 47 s  |
Earlier cold recoveries in the same boots (dump 22 at 64489, dump 23 at
1110) were not followed by a panic; s2idle resumes without a cold recovery
never were.  A stray write to a random page fits: usually it lands somewhere
harmless.

* Ruled out so far
- The Thunderbolt DMA engines (00:0d.2/.3, no driver) come back from D3cold
  with their saved command register 0x0003: bus mastering off.
- xhci0's own DMA memory is intact in dump 22: DCBAA[0] = scratchpad array,
  array = the 34 attach-time scratchpad pages, rings where the softc says.
- hdaudio keeps its CORB/RIRB across sleep and reprograms the same buffers.
- dma-buf mmap hands the process the GEM object's aobj (UVM-managed pages).

* Experiments (2026-10-30, kernel #208, on AC)
userland/memwatch.c fills ~3.5 GB (22 % of RAM) with a per-word pattern,
locks it, runs a trigger, and reports changed pages; userland/zexp.sh holds
the triggers, log in /var/log/zexp.log.

| run | trigger                                             | rounds | changed pages |
|-----+-----------------------------------------------------+--------+---------------|
| A   | none (10 s idle)                                    |      3 |             0 |
| B   | =usb on usbc; usb off usbc= (xhci0 cold recovery)    |     10 |             0 |

B ran ten real cold recoveries ("power was lost (ACPI), cold recovery",
"empty controller restarted") without a single changed word, so the TCSS
power cycle and xhci0's cold path alone are not the writer (a writer that
hit a random page each time would have been seen with ~92 % probability).

The dumps' logs show what B left out: in all three boots the PCH controller
xhci1 was on as well, with devices.  Dump 21: =usb on= at runtime, camera
re-attached, Bluetooth firmware loaded, bt-headphones (pad0) 8 s later,
panic 36 s after.  Dumps 22 and 23: a GenesysLogic USB2 hub pair with a
keyboard (uhidev) and in 22 a USB stick (umass0) on uhub3 port 5 (the USB-C
port's USB2 lines go to the PCH controller); in 22 the resume logged
"xhci1: xhci_set_dequeue: endpoint 0x0: timed out".  Boots with =usb off=
never had an xhci0 cold recovery, so "xhci0 cold" and "xhci1 on with
devices" always came together.

| run | trigger                                             | rounds | changed pages |
|-----+-----------------------------------------------------+--------+---------------|
| E   | =bluetooth on= (both controllers, firmware load, bthub), =btconfig ubt0 up/down=, =usb off= | 10 | 0 |

E repeated the dump 21 sequence (xhci0 cold recovery, xhci1 resume with
camera, fingerprint reader and ubt, firmware download) ten times: clean.

Timing points elsewhere too: kernel 206 (build #208) became the default on
10-24 22:13 and all three panics (10-26, 10-28, 10-29) are on it; the weeks
before, with the same USB code and many s2idle cycles, had none.  206 changed
the GPU side (gen12 AUX-table invalidation, the HDC flush bit, the dma-buf
poll race).  A GPU still writing through a mapping of pages that were freed
(a render-target clear writes zeros) would leave exactly a zeroed page that
the allocator has meanwhile given to someone else, and the fixed memwatch
region cannot see it: its pages were never GPU pages.  memwatch therefore
got MEMWATCH_ROLL=MB, a region taken from the free list at the end of every
round and checked at the end of the next.

| run | trigger                                                | rounds | changed pages      |
|-----+--------------------------------------------------------+--------+--------------------|
| F   | usb on + bt up, timed s2idle (20 s), roll 1500 MB       |      6 | 0 (fresh: 0)       |
| G   | VA-API decode + scale_vaapi churn (ffmpeg8), roll 3000 MB |   20 | 0 (fresh: 0)       |

F reproduced the state of dumps 22 and 23 (both controllers on, Bluetooth
up, TCSS cut during the freeze, xhci0 cold recovery and xhci1 warm resume
after it) six times: clean.  So no USB trigger reproduces the wipe in short
runs; the real panics needed hours of desktop use around a few resumes.

* Suspect: GPU TLB entries outliving the pages (CVE-2022-0330)
The i915 in NetBSD 11 is Linux 5.6.  Linux 7938d61591d3 ("Flush TLBs
before releasing backing store", 2022, CVE-2022-0330) and its gen12 fix
04aa64375f48 (masked video/enhance invalidation registers, CVE-2022-4139)
are missing: when a GEM object's pages go back to UVM nothing invalidates
the engine TLBs, so the GPU can keep writing through a cached translation
into a page that the allocator has meanwhile given to a pool, a process or
the file system.  This matches every finding in the dumps:

- the victim is a page with an ordinary, unrelated current owner;
- nothing in memory refers to it (no PA, PFN, PTE or direct-map pointer
  anywhere in dump 22): a hardware TLB entry leaves no trace in RAM, a stale
  CPU pointer or USB descriptor would;
- whole pages of zeros are what a cleared render target or a transparent
  WebRender surface writes;
- Firefox (WebRender, VA-API, dma-buf sharing with Mahogany) was running in
  all three; kernel 191 (10-19) started freeing exported dma-buf objects
  that used to leak, which multiplied the frees of GPU-visible pages.

Kernel 211 ports it: i915_vma_bind marks the object (I915_BO_WAS_BOUND_BIT),
__i915_gem_object_unset_pages invalidates the TLBs of every engine through
GEN12_{GFX,VD,VE,BLT}_TLB_INV_CR (masked bits for VD/VE) and waits up to
4 ms for completion, serialised with GT reset under uncore->lock and skipped
when wedged.  It does not wake a parked GT (nothing is in flight and every
request starts with a TLB-invalidating flush), so the idle cost is zero.

Measured with dtrace (fbt) on kernel 211/213:
- fresh boot, 10 s VA-API decode + scale: 118 calls, 6 full invalidations
  (30 engine waits = 5 engines x 6), 8-100 us each; the rest found the GT
  parked;
- idle desktop, 20 s: 0 calls;
- during s2stress load after a resume: 295-600 calls per 10-20 s, all with
  the GT parked: it parks and unparks ~89 times a second during VA-API
  decode (4500 execbuffers in 10 s), and the frees come after retirement.
  Upstream skips a parked GT the same way (with_intel_gt_pm_if_awake).

* Kernel 213 stress (2026-10-30 03:27-04:39)
s2stress.sh 20 30 180 with both USB controllers on and Bluetooth up (the
state of all three panics), alongside memwatch 3000 MB locked + 2000 MB
fresh region rolled every 15 s: 20 freeze/resume cycles, each followed by
3 min of audio DMA, map churn, gefs writes, VA-API decode and network; 222
memwatch rounds, 0 changed pages; no panic, fault, assertion or TLB timeout
in dmesg.  This supports kernel 213 but does not prove the fix: the
corruption hit roughly once a day of normal use and s2stress was never run
on #208 for comparison.  If it comes back on 213, the dump plus the
memwatch fresh region are the next tools; the USB experiments above remain
the reference for what is already excluded.
