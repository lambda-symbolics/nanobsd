#+title: xHCI power-loss resume and ubt runtime parking

* Scope

Kernel 174 meets all three laptop goals, reported 2026-10-15 at 21:00:

- =usb off=: TCSS cold under the suspended, attached xhci0; 1.20 W
  panel-off on battery, PC10 98%.
- PCH on, radio up and runtime-parked: 1.20 W, matching the USB-off
  baseline; inquiry wakes the radio. The 240-second kernel 171 measurement
  recorded PC10 97%.
- Lid suspend with USB on and the radio up: empty TXHC goes D3cold,
  PC10 residency is 98%, SLP_S0 advances by 197978, and cold recovery and
  Bluetooth inquiry work after wake.

Use the matching =kernel/dev/acpi/acpi.c=, =kernel/dev/usb/xhci.c=,
=kernel/dev/usb/xhcivar.h= and =kernel/dev/usb/ubt.c= changes with the
D3cold ACPI backend. Install the updated =userland/usb= script too:
controller resume alone does not resume its USB children.

Cold recovery currently requires no allocated slots, runtime parking
ownership or downstream USB devices on either bus. ACPI checks the
controller's activity and =lispbsd-usb-populated= before cutting TCSS power.
A plugged USB-C device therefore keeps TCSS warm at lid close. Populated
cold recovery needs the lifecycle and policy work described below.

* Detect and restart an empty controller

=acpi_adr_power()= restores the D3cold power resources and PCI configuration,
then sets =lispbsd-power-lost=true= on the attached function. In =xhci_resume()=,
consume that property into =sc_state_lost= before probing the controller.
This is the cold-recovery trigger on the tested Tiger Lake hardware: CRS
after power loss reports success, with no RSS or SRE, but the controller
has lost its working state. Commit =3dba156= added the explicit handoff.

=xhci_resume_check()= runs before resume writes an operational register.
PCI must already have restored BARs, command and interrupt configuration
through its PMF bus-resume callback. The probe rejects inaccessible MMIO,
checks the attach-time layout, waits for USBSTS.CNR to clear and requires a
saved suspend snapshot. HSE/HCE also request cold recovery.

Zero or changed DCBAAP, USBCMD, CONFIG and ERSTBA do not distinguish D3hot
from power loss. Without the ACPI tag or another reset requirement, restore
the saved registers and attempt CRS. An RSS timeout requests cold recovery;
it indicates failed restore completion, not proof of physical power loss.
=sc_state_lost= is cleared only after successful recovery, so consuming the
property does not lose the reset requirement if resume fails.

Register offsets, slot/interrupter/port and scratchpad counts, and
HCCPARAMS1 must match. HCIVERSION changes are informational: log them with
=aprint_debug_dev()=, then check the actual layout and snapshot validity.
Zero or all-ones HCIVERSION reads are rejected as unavailable MMIO.

On the tested Tiger Lake machine, HCIVERSION reads 0x0110 after every tested
PCI D3hot cycle, versus 0x0120 at attach. Operational registers also read zero
after D3hot; the warm path rewrites them before CRS. Neither observation is
proof of cold power loss. Commits =34c10c3= and =f9b3987= remove the version
and register-equality checks that blocked warm resume in kernels 165 and 166.

=xhci_init()= owns allocations, mappings, locks, CVs, slots and callouts.
After HCRST and the vendor hook, =xhci_init_hw()= reuses those allocations:

1. Clear command/event rings and cookies; reset indices, cycle states and
   halted flags. Synchronise DMA memory and event-ring read ownership.
2. Clear scratchpads and DCBAA slot entries; restore the scratchpad-array
   pointer in DCBAA[0] and rebuild the event segment table.
3. Program DNCTRL, DCBAAP, CONFIG, CRCR, ERSTSZ, ERSTBA and ERDP.
4. Use =xhci_start()= for IMAN, IMOD and Run/Stop; wait for HCH to clear and
   check fatal status. Reset itself now waits for HCH before HCRST.
5. Power ports without acknowledging pending change bits and wait for
   power to settle.

* Root hubs and PMF ordering

Retain the software USB buses and root hubs. After successful cold restart,
mark every root-hub port =up_reattach= and call =usb_needs_explore()= without
=sc_lock= or =sc_rhlock=. The normal USB event thread then checks each port,
including connections made while power was off. An exploration request
alone would not pass =uhub_explore()='s port-change/reattach filter.

Relevant NetBSD-11 contracts:

- =pmf_device_resume()= holds the device PMF lock. Its locked implementation
  resumes bus, driver, then class.
- =usb_attach()= and =uhub_attach()= register NULL PMF callbacks, so there is
  no automatic resume rescan to rely on.
- =usb_discover()= enters with KERNEL_LOCK and the bus lock, then drops the
  bus lock for hub exploration. =uhub_explore()= requires the USB event
  thread. =usb_needs_explore()= acquires the bus lock itself.
- =usb_disconnect_port()= performs driver detach and device teardown;
  =usb_detach()= also joins its event thread. These paths may need root
  control requests and HCD commands. They cannot safely run behind the
  resume locks and command gate used here.
- =deviter_init()/device_exists_at()= use a generation snapshot. Attach and
  detach during iteration are permitted, but replacement devices are outside
  that snapshot and removed devices can still belong to it.

No child detach/reattach occurs inside the PMF callback. Queuing work under
KERNEL_LOCK does not establish a barrier after the entire ACPI resume walk:
sleeping may release that lock.

* Runtime parking

The idle policy accepts zero non-control pipes, or open pipes belonging
only to active ubt children. Require at least one child for that opt-in.
The ubt driver registers its PMF handlers at attach; xHCI checks the result
of =pmf_device_suspend()= rather than calling the private registration query.
HID policy and the direct-root-port restriction also apply. The callout
queues candidates; inspect descriptors and subdevices in the non-MPSAFE USB
task under KERNEL_LOCK, not in softint context.

For open-pipe parking, require remote-wakeup capability and successful
SET_FEATURE. Suspend the drivers through =PMF_Q_SELF= before requesting U3.
Track the attempted child prefix, including a failed suspend that may have
installed a suspensor, so rollback owns exactly those PMF requests. Recheck
activity and runtime admission after callbacks. Rearm inbound pipes only
after confirming an enabled, connected U0 link.

Defer endpoint doorbells throughout parking and wake. Restore U0, resume
the parked drivers to queue inbound reads, then release pending doorbells.
Retain failed PMF resumes and deferred I/O for the next idle-scan retry,
even with autosuspend disabled. Serialize runtime tasks and synchronous
wake-for-open with a per-slot owner; permit recursive pipe opens by the
PMF resume callback on that same thread. The ubt event-pipe reopen preserves
its parked state on failure and retries with a NULL pipe pointer. Disabling
ubt clears its parked flag before a later enable cycle.

Before system suspend installs =sc_suspender=, pause runtime admission,
halt the callout, remove queued reruns, join executing tasks and drain
synchronous wake-for-open operations. Wait without either xHCI sleep lock
while commands needed by existing runtime work can complete. Unbinding a
slot likewise closes task admission and removes a rerun before joining.

After successful warm resume, replay pending doorbells for ordinary slots.
For runtime-parked drivers, which system PMF suspend skipped as inactive,
queue resume work and keep I/O deferred until their inbound reads are queued.
Cold recovery has no owned slots to replay. Both slot walks include IDs 1
through =sc_maxslots=.

A failed resume preserves the snapshot and cold-recovery requirement, gates
interrupts/root requests/doorbells and leaves runtime admission paused.
A failed suspend reopens runtime admission, but retains the pre-existing
=sc_suspendresume_failed= command-error policy. This is not a complete
suspend rollback: PMF does not call driver resume after failed driver
suspend. The separate =sc_resume_failed= flag avoids extending that legacy
command failure into a new blanket traffic gate on suspend failure.

* Populated cold recovery: lifecycle and policy

Removing the empty-slot check is insufficient. HCRST invalidates hardware
slots while software pipes and devices still refer to them. PMF resumes the
controller before its descendants, so those drivers can submit I/O before
an asynchronous root-hub rescan retires the old tree. An =up_reattach= flag
alone does not close that window.

Choose the device semantics before extending the ACPI cold-off policy:

- Disconnect/re-enumerate: retire eligible downstream trees before the power
  cut and before the PMF device snapshot. Use the USB event-thread lifecycle,
  with command processing available and without xHCI sleep locks held. Gate
  new enumeration while preparing the cold transition. Require successful
  retirement of the whole tree before authorising power removal. Initially
  exclude storage and unsupported drivers, including those behind hubs; on
  refusal retain the warm path. Applications would observe a disconnect and
  new attachment, and existing device handles would not survive.
- Preserve device identity and open handles: add USB-core reset recovery that
  verifies the same devices, restores addressing/configuration/interfaces and
  rebuilds endpoint state before child PMF callbacks or I/O resume. Mounted
  storage needs this kind of recovery rather than forced detach. The
  filesystem suspension barrier alone does not make detach safe.

If retirement is deferred until after power loss, add software-only
abort/close/free paths for invalid slots, suppress old child resume callbacks,
join runtime and endpoint tasks, and provide an explicit post-PMF handoff
after =deviter_release()=. A USB task queued by =xhci_resume()= is not that
barrier. Old slots must be fully retired before new enumeration can reuse IDs.

The current =lispbsd-usb-populated= property counts bound slots; it is a
conservative exclusion, not a completed cold-preparation transaction.
Keep power-cut eligibility separate from reset recovery, with an explicit
success/failure handoff and rollback. Neither populated path is implemented.

* Kernel 165 result and build corrections

The 12:00 entry in =/root/astra/pc3-results.org= records the first
=usb on pch= failure on kernel 165: a normal D3hot -> D0 transition produced
=capability layout changed (HCIVERSION 0120 -> 0110)=. The version check
rejected warm resume, followed by =xhci_set_dequeue= timeouts and
=btconfig up= returning EIO. Bluetooth parking could not be tested.

Commit =51350c9= supplied four build corrections before that laptop run:

- Rename local =version= to =hciver= to avoid shadowing the kernel global.
- Rename local =cold= to =coldres= for the same reason.
- Assign =XHCI_HCS2_MAXSPBUF(hcs2)= to =maxspbuf= before comparing it. The
  macro has no outer parentheses; direct use in the compound condition
  changes the comparison's meaning through operator precedence.
- Remove =device_pmf_is_registered()= from the ubt eligibility predicate.
  It is not a public NetBSD-11 driver API. Use ubt's attach-time registration
  and the public PMF suspend/resume calls.

Source review of =51350c9= and the HCIVERSION correction =34c10c3= is complete.

* Kernel 166 restore decision and review

The 12:20 entry in =/root/astra/pc3-results.org= records DCBAAP and USBCMD
reading zero after an ordinary D3hot resume on xhci1. Kernel 166 incorrectly
classified this as lost state and attempted cold recovery. The populated
controller failed the empty-slot guard, so the helper refused recovery before
issuing HCRST. The failed-resume gates then prevented normal use.

Commit =f9b3987= removes the register-equality trigger. The probe now requests
cold recovery only on HSE/HCE; otherwise it tries the normal register restore
and CRS sequence, with an RSS timeout as a fallback trigger. This addresses
the observed false positive. Kernel 167 was building when the result arrived.

The [[https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/technical-specifications/extensible-host-controler-interface-usb-xhci.pdf][xHCI 1.2 specification]], sections 4.23.1 and 4.23.2.1, supports trying the
restore: core-well registers reset on D3hot -> D0, and saved state can be
restored across power events. Timeout indicates failed completion, not its
cause. Section 4.23.2.1 also requires a corrupt-state restore to terminate
with RSS clear and SRE set. Thus RSS clear alone does not establish success.

The new timeout fallback has the required lock/admission ordering: runtime
work is paused, =sc_rhlock= is held, and =sc_lock= is released before calling
=xhci_resume_cold()=. The helper rechecks slot and topology ownership before
reset. Populated recovery is still refused; success alone releases deferred
I/O and restarts runtime admission.

One recovery gap is present in =f9b3987=: an RSS-clear/SRE-set result reaches
the late SRE check and returns failure without cold fallback. For an empty
controller, the next recovery change should handle SRE alongside timeout,
checking fresh HSE/HCE and rejecting all-ones MMIO before starting Run/Stop.
The current timeout loop also treats all-ones USBSTS as RSS still set; the
subsequent reset helper's CNR check rejects persistently inaccessible MMIO.

* Kernel 171 parking result and child resume

The 13:10 entry in =/root/astra/pc3-results.org= and
[[file:tcss-d3cold.org::*Bluetooth radio parking, kernels 165-171 (afternoon/evening 2026-10-15)][the TCSS hardware notes]] record:

- On kernels 167+, the radio parks, inquiry wakes it (=wakes=1=, =resumes=2=),
  and inquiry works after freeze/resume with an already parked radio.
- On kernel 171, PCH on, ubt0 up and parked, and TCSS cold yielded PC10 98%.
  The Wh measurement window was still running at report time.
- After a plain =usb on pch=, ubt0 initially kept its boot-time drvctl PMF
  suspension. =drvctl -R= resumes the named device and its ancestors, not its
  descendants: DRVRESUMEDEV is issued without DEVPM_F_SUBTREE. The active-child
  predicate therefore correctly refused to runtime-park ubt0.
- Commit =125f41d= adds =resume_tree()= to the USB script. Resume the controller
  and then its descendants, leaves last, including when the controller is
  already in D0. This releases the child suspension left by boot-time
  =usb off=. The parking diagnostics added through kernel 171 identified
  the inactive child and its PMF suspensor.

* Kernel 174 cold recovery and default policy

The 21:00 entry in =/root/astra/pc3-results.org= confirms the three results
listed in Scope. Empty attached-controller cold recovery works from kernel
172 onward through the ACPI power-loss property, not through CRS failure.
The successful lid test on kernel 174 also exercised cold recovery after
SLP_S0 and Bluetooth inquiry after wake.

Commit =0c98e67= changed the default policy:

- xHCI publishes =lispbsd-usb-populated= when binding/unbinding device slots.
- For TXHC, =ADRPWR_NODRIVER= permits an absent driver or a suspended,
  unpopulated controller. An active or populated controller is skipped.
- TXHC participates in the s2idle power table; suspend it through PMF before
  removing the shared TCSS power resources.
- =usb off= suspends xhci0 without detaching it. =usb on= restores power and
  PCI configuration, cold-resumes the controller, then resumes the USB tree.
  Use =USB_TCSS_DETACH=1= for the earlier suspend/detach/rescan path, or
  =USB_TCSS_D3=0= for D3hot only.

The earlier =hw.acpi.sleep.idle_d3_exp= mask =0x4= override is unnecessary
for normal empty-controller cold recovery. It bypasses the active/populated
guard and is not a populated-recovery enable switch.

The package sits at PC8 for roughly the first eight minutes after boot,
regardless of USB state. Measure after ten minutes of uptime. The transient
briefly implicated xHCI MSI enable; that change was reverted in =6b1b135=.

* Further validation

Populated recovery needs an agreed device policy and implementation before
hardware testing. Cover direct USB2/USB3 devices, hubs, removal or replacement
while asleep, failed recovery, and repeated cold/warm cycles. For a
re-enumeration policy, verify pre-cut rejection of every excluded subtree;
for identity-preserving recovery, verify open handles and storage integrity.
Record power and SLP_S0 only after the boot-transient window.

Keep the existing SRE-only failure case separate: kernel 174 returns failure
without cold fallback for RSS-clear/SRE-set. The explicit ACPI power-loss tag
avoids that restore path for the tested TCSS cold transition.
